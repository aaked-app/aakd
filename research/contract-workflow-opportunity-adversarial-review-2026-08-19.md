## 1. Executive verdict

**VALUE JUDGMENT — High confidence.** Stage 3 represents the broad status quo fairly enough to enter a challenge gate, but none of `T1`–`T6` has earned an opportunity conclusion. The corpus establishes that the workflows and failure mechanisms can exist; it does not establish their prevalence, causal dominance, reachable buyer, willingness to pay (`WTP`), or repeatable economics in the proposed population. `H0` therefore remains an active competitor rather than a ceremonial null.

**VALUE JUDGMENT — High confidence.** `T5` is not presently a testable category: “a narrow recurring review/extraction task” still pools materially different contract families, outputs and critical errors. It is **rejected in its current form before primary validation**. `T6` is also **rejected in its current form unless** a preregistered test can separate repeatable assurance from bespoke consulting. These rejections are research dispositions, not claims that the underlying problems are unreal.

**Stage 4 recommendation:** **PASS**, conditional on freezing and applying the protocols in §12 without repair after results. The review passes because each thesis now has a fair rival and a test capable of returning `KILL`; it does not mean any thesis passes its own test.

## 3. Inversion: assume the opportunity program fails

If the program fails after apparently positive discovery, the most plausible explanation is not that every interviewee lied. It is that research confused one or more of the following:

1. a real but rare incident with a recurring budgeted job;
2. a coordination or authority failure with an information-system failure;
3. workflow existence with an unmet need;
4. user frustration with buyer urgency and authority;
5. a vendor story with independent performance evidence;
6. an enterprise/regulated edge case with a 51–500-employee US/UK reference class;
7. a faster local step with improved end-to-end business outcome;
8. a prototype-friendly task with a trustworthy, administrable operating process;
9. services revenue with repeatable, defensible economics; or
10. interview agreement with costly revealed behavior.

This inversion makes three controls non-negotiable: test a recent episode against a working counterexample; observe the current process and its existing-system remedy before proposing displacement; and require a hard paid commitment before an opportunity survives.

## 4. `T1` — Vendor renewal/notice decision control

### Strongest incumbent/null case

**STEELMAN — Medium confidence.** A named owner, a clean executed-contract folder, a simple register, and a 90–120-day review cadence may be proportionate. `CFA-L` R05 describes Excel plus Power Automate as the fix in the same thread where R04 reports expirations, late renewals and outdated terms. R10–R17 and R54 document registers, planners, project boards and suite reminders that users sometimes consider sufficient. GitLab's enterprise practice—quarterly rolling review with the business owner and renewal work at least 90 days ahead—also shows that governance and cadence are part of the control, although it does not prove a mid-market tool need ([GitLab procurement handbook](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/finance/procurement/)).

### Adversarial findings

- **Unsupported inference — Medium confidence:** the jump from “a reminder is insufficient in some episodes” to a distinct recurring decision-readiness problem in 51–500-employee US/UK firms. Current observations lack verified size/geography, recurrence denominators and counterfactuals.
- **Actor/buyer/incentive failure — High confidence:** procurement or an analyst maintains dates and context; the business owner may prefer continuity; finance captures savings; IT bears integration/admin; no one may own the budget for an avoided renewal.
- **Status-quo adequacy — Medium confidence:** a disciplined register and cadence can make the governing date, owner and decision visible at low cost. A process intervention is the correct first rival.
- **Adoption/review burden — Medium confidence:** maintaining usage, performance, ownership, amendment and notice data can exceed the avoided-loss value; alert fatigue encourages silent bypass.
- **Trust/legal risk — Medium confidence:** a stale notice address, amended notice period or incorrect auto-renewal interpretation can create false confidence; human verification remains necessary for consequential notices.
- **Suite/displacement risk — High confidence:** procurement suites and enterprise CLM already claim renewal, supplier and obligation workflows; the buyer may configure a system it already pays for.
- **WTP/defensibility gap — High confidence:** no independent target-population payment, repeated use or defensibility evidence exists.
- **Metric gaming/second-order effect — Medium confidence:** “review completed before deadline” can be satisfied by perfunctory approval; earlier supplier contact may alter pricing tactics; more tracked renewals can increase apparent workload without changing waste.

**Most diagnostic crux:** do recent renewal failures persist after a 30-day owner/register/cadence correction because governing context and authority still fail to converge?

**Disconfirming test:** run the process-first control in qualified organizations, then audit all renewal decisions due during the observation window. If the control resolves timeliness and correctness at low admin cost, or buyers will not pay after a documented residual failure, `T1` fails.

## 5. `T2` — Sell-side signature-to-booking/fulfillment continuity

### Strongest incumbent/null case

**STEELMAN — High confidence.** The receiving system—not a separate contract layer—should own booking, provisioning and billing state. GitLab's public enterprise workflow puts legal requests and approvals in Salesforce, uses authorized signatories and DocuSign, and sends nonstandard paper through Deal Desk, revenue, order-management and finance gates before booking/fulfilment ([GitLab order processing](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/sales/field-operations/order-processing/)). Salesforce and adjacent revenue suites explicitly position quote-to-cash controls. Where exceptions are few, a booking checklist plus accountable RevOps owner may be adequate.

### Adversarial findings

- **Unsupported inference — High confidence:** workflow adjacency does not show that contract-state discontinuity causes material delay in the target cohort. The medium-company Lenus and Placemakr examples are vendor-published narratives; GitLab is enterprise and operator-authored, not an effect study.
- **Segment/geography gap — High confidence:** no independent 51–500-employee US/UK episode with measured consequence or WTP appears in the corpus.
- **Actor/buyer/incentive failure — High confidence:** sales is rewarded for bookings and may bypass controls; legal approves risk but does not own revenue data; finance bears control risk; RevOps and IT bear integration cost; the presumed buyer may not control all four.
- **Status-quo adequacy — High confidence:** CRM/order-management configuration, required fields and a close checklist may address the handoff without displacement.
- **Implementation/adoption burden — High confidence:** mapping nonstandard clauses to operational fields is exception-heavy; a hard gate may delay good deals, while a soft gate may be ignored.
- **False-positive/correction cost — High confidence:** an incorrectly inferred billing, renewal or provisioning term can propagate into CRM, ERP, invoices and delivery. Every downstream copy increases correction cost and audit scope.
- **Security/privacy/legal veto — Medium confidence:** customer paper, DPA and security schedules can be sensitive; data routing, retention and model/provider terms may trigger legal/security veto.
- **Suite response/defensibility gap — High confidence:** CRM, CPQ, revenue and enterprise CLM vendors can absorb the workflow; no independent evidence shows a durable advantage or paid demand.
- **Metric gaming/second-order effect — Medium confidence:** teams can reduce “signature-to-booking time” by classifying exceptions away or booking before validation, shifting defects into billing/support. A new gate can move rather than remove the bottleneck.

**Most diagnostic crux:** in a deal-level audit, what fraction of nonstandard executed agreements produce contract-caused downstream exceptions after ordinary workload and suite-configuration failures are excluded?

**Disconfirming test:** configure the existing CRM/order checklist first and prospectively audit the next nonstandard deals. If residual contract-caused exceptions are below the frozen threshold, native configuration resolves them, or receiving teams refuse the operating change, `T2` fails.

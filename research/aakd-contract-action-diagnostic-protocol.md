# Aakd Contract Action Diagnostic — validation protocol

**Status:** preparation only. This is not an outreach list, product promise, security claim, or offer sent to any account.

## Purpose

Falsify—not confirm—the only remaining Cloud hypothesis:

> A lower-midmarket, non-MSP service organization with a specific failure in its current contract/SharePoint/CLM stack will pay for a human-reviewed, cited action ledger from 25 executed agreements and prefer a managed service to self-hosting.

The protocol follows the decision gates in `aakd-cloud-icp-evidence-2026-08-16.md` and the H3 restrictions in `aakd-customer-first-clm-cycle-2026-08-16.md`. A generic need for reminders, storage, AI chat, or contract management is a disqualification, not a positive signal.

Operational intake, consent, corpus handling, deletion, pilot receipts, repeat
cycles, and review decisions are defined in the [Phase 0 evidence-intake
checklist](gates/phase-0-evidence-intake-checklist.md). That checklist is
mandatory for evidence to enter the ledger; it does not itself count as
customer evidence.

## Eligibility screen

Proceed only if all answers are true:

1. The organization has at least 25 executed agreements in one comparable workflow (for example, SOWs, supplier agreements, client service contracts, or amendments).
2. A recent post-signature episode had a concrete consequence: missed notice, unwanted renewal, unowned commitment, billing/delivery error, audit issue, or material manual workload.
3. The participant can name the current system(s) and why they did not prevent that episode.
4. The participant can introduce or is the person who controls the workflow budget.
5. A redacted corpus can be provided under an agreed data-handling process.

Do not proceed when a PSA, ATS, CLM, Microsoft configuration, or project-management tool already produces the desired owned and cited actions; record that outcome as a disconfirmation.

## Conversation guide

Ask every question verbatim enough to compare interviews. Record the answer as a direct quote, the evidence artifact offered, and the interviewer’s separate interpretation.

| Question | Strong qualifying answer | Disqualifying answer | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| “Tell me about the last contract commitment that created avoidable work or cost after signature.” | Names one agreement, event, date, owner gap and consequence. | General complaint or only a pre-signature/drafting issue. | Tests a real post-signature episode rather than abstract feature interest. |
| “Where did the agreement, its deadline and its owner each live at the time?” | Names two or more systems and a manual reconciliation step. | One system already provides reliable ownership, dates, source text and escalation. | Identifies the actual workflow boundary and incumbent. |
| “What did you do to fix it, and what did that cost in time, money or risk?” | Describes paid tool, consultant, staff time or measurable consequence. | “We set a reminder and it is fine.” | Tests visible cost and a credible budget route. |
| “Why could your current CLM, SharePoint/Microsoft setup, PSA/ATS or project tool not do this?” | Identifies a specific missing capability or adoption/integration failure that survives fact-checking. | Cannot name a limitation, or says configuration would solve it. | Prevents Aakd from becoming an unnecessary replacement. |
| “Who owns this operational result, and who can fund a remedy?” | Names a role, decision process and budget path. | “Everyone” or “legal/IT will decide later.” | Separates user sympathy from economic authority. |
| “If 25 redacted agreements became cited actions, owners and notice windows in one business day, what would you use next?” | States a recurring decision, task destination or next contract batch. | Wants a one-time cleanup or generic repository. | Tests repeatability and a destination workflow. |
| “Which would you choose: self-hosted community Aakd, or managed Aakd Cloud with setup, upgrades, backups and support? Why?” | Explicitly prefers managed operations and names a practical reason. | Prefers self-hosting/local only, or requires unspecified compliance. | Tests Cloud preference separately from product value. |
| “What would make this a non-starter?” | Names data boundary, accuracy, approval, integration, price or liability requirement. | No concrete answer. | Produces an explicit falsification condition and product gap. |

## Fixed-scope pilot definition

**Input:** 25 redacted, executed agreements of one comparable type; no production access, no automatic updates to external systems, and no promise of legal advice.

**Output:** a human-reviewed export containing each proposed action, source text and page, trigger/date, confidence, proposed owner, review decision, and unresolved ambiguity. Every entry must say either **confirmed**, **corrected**, or **unable to determine**.

**Success threshold:** within one business day, the customer judges at least 80% of proposed actions useful enough to assign, correct or export; two organizations prepay this identical scope and request a second cycle.

**Immediate stop conditions:**

- The customer only needs a repository or simple reminder.
- Existing software can produce the output after routine configuration.
- The user will not involve a budget owner.
- The corpus cannot be safely redacted or handled under a defined boundary.
- The expected value depends on legal advice, autonomous decisions, unsupported compliance certification, or integrations Aakd cannot honestly deliver.

## Evidence record for every conversation

Store no contract text, credentials, or private personal data in this research record. Link only to approved internal notes or a redacted output location.

| Field | Required record |
|---|---|
| Account type / size band | Participant-provided description, not inferred from LinkedIn. |
| Participant role | Exact title and relationship to the workflow. |
| Workflow and agreement type | The concrete agreement family and post-signature process. |
| Incident quote | Verbatim participant wording plus date/context. |
| Existing stack | Named tools, configuration, owner and claimed limitation. |
| Consequence | Money, time, delay, risk or audit impact; label unknown if unquantified. |
| Buyer and budget path | Named role/process; never infer authority. |
| Deployment preference | Self-hosted / managed / neither, with exact reason. |
| Pilot response | Prepay / unpaid only / no / unknown. |
| Disqualifier | Exact reason, including incumbent coverage. |
| Evidence quality | Firsthand / secondhand / vendor claim / unknown. |

## Go / no-go scoring

An interview is **qualified** only when it earns all four core checks: concrete incident, current-stack limitation, economic path, and a repeatable next batch. A pilot is **commercially qualified** only when it is prepaid and the buyer chooses a managed option for a stated operational reason.

| Check | Pass | Fail |
|---|---|---|
| Concrete incident | Specific agreement, workflow and consequence | Generic interest or pre-signature-only problem |
| Incumbent failure | Verifiable limitation in current system | Existing tool solves it or customer will configure it |
| Buyer path | Named economic owner and next decision | Unnamed committee or no budget path |
| Repeatable outcome | Next batch / operating cycle identified | One-time cleanup only |
| Cloud preference | Managed choice with reason | Self-hosted only, neither, or requirement not yet deliverable |

Do not select an ICP from interview enthusiasm. The full research gate still requires two prepaid, repeated pilots and corroborating evidence across comparable organizations.

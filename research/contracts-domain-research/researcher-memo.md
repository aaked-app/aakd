# Contracts in operating companies: domain research for Aakd

**Research date:** 2026-08-19  
**Geographic emphasis:** United States and England & Wales (with explicit jurisdiction notes)  
**Purpose:** founder-grade domain research for Aakd's provisional 50–500 employee US/UK ICP and cited post-signature-action hypothesis  
**Status:** research synthesis, not legal advice

## Reading conventions and evidence standard

- **FACT** means a statement directly supported by the linked authority or a named operator's documented account.
- **INFERENCE** means a conclusion drawn from facts; it is useful for product discovery but is not yet customer validation.
- **OPEN GAP** means the public record does not answer the question reliably.
- **Primary / authoritative** includes legislation, regulators, courts, standards setters, public filings, and a company's own operating handbook.
- **Practitioner standard** includes professional bodies such as World Commerce & Contracting (WorldCC) and the Association of Corporate Counsel (ACC).
- **Vendor case study** is customer-attributed evidence published by a seller. It can document a workflow, but its outcome figures are not treated as neutral benchmarks.

## Executive answer

**FACT — High confidence.** A contract is not merely a signed PDF. In US terminology it is the legally enforceable obligation resulting from the parties' agreement; core common-law requirements commonly include mutual assent, consideration, capacity, and legality, while state law and transaction-specific statutes vary. The Uniform Commercial Code (UCC) expressly distinguishes the parties' “agreement” from the “contract,” meaning the total legal obligation produced by that agreement and applicable law. In England and Wales, an enforceable commercial contract ordinarily turns on agreement, consideration, intention to create legal relations, sufficient certainty, and any applicable formalities; acceptance, conduct, and performance can create a binding contract even without the anticipated final signature. [Cornell LII, “contract,” current page accessed 2026-08-19](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/contract); [UCC §1-201(b)(12), accessed 2026-08-19](https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/1/1-201); [UK Cabinet Office procurement guidance, updated 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/procurement-act-2023-guidance-documents-procure-phase/guidance-contract-award-notices-and-standstill-html); [RTS Flexible Systems v Müller, UKSC 14, 2010](https://www.supremecourt.uk/cases/uksc-2009-0048)

**FACT — High confidence.** The operational object is usually an **agreement family**, not one document: an MSA may be supplemented by service agreements, schedules, statements of work (SOWs), order forms, data/security exhibits, change orders, amendments, and notices, with negotiated precedence rules. The SEC-filed Sabre–DXC outsourcing agreement expressly defines “Agreement” collectively and adds service agreements, schedules, procedures manuals, change orders, privacy/security requirements, governance, acceptance, and termination-assistance machinery. [Sabre–DXC amended and restated MSA, filed 2021](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1597033/000159703321000022/exhibit10103masterservices.htm)

**FACT — High confidence.** Signing is the midpoint, not the end. A defensible lifecycle includes demand and strategy; intake and triage; counterparty diligence; drafting; review and negotiation; approvals and authority; execution; mobilisation and handoff; performance, obligations, invoices and evidence; change/claims/disputes; renewal/termination; exit/transition; and record retention. The UK Government's contract-management standard similarly covers design, procurement/mobilisation, managing delivery, change control, stakeholder/risk management, and exit/transition. [UK Government Contract Management Professional Standards v2, 2019](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/contract-management); [2026 foundation takeaway guide](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/civil-service-helping-you-with-managing-suppliers-and-contracts/contract-management-training-accreditation-foundation-level-takeaway-guide-html)

**INFERENCE — Medium confidence.** Company size changes the **distribution of ownership and control density** more reliably than it changes the underlying lifecycle. A small company may have a founder or functional lead creating, approving, signing, filing, and remembering obligations; a medium company adds thresholds, specialist reviews, and multiple systems; an enterprise adds segmented contract types, delegated authority, legal operations, formal controls, and complex agreement families. Public examples below support this pattern, but they are not a representative sample.

**INFERENCE — Medium confidence.** For Aakd's provisional 50–500 employee ICP, the most credible initial pain is not generic “AI contract review.” It is the failure to turn an executed, sometimes multi-document agreement into reviewed actions, named owners, conditions, deadlines, evidence, and escalation in the team's operational system. This is consistent with regulatory requirements, public company workflows, and customer-attributed case studies, but remains a falsifiable hypothesis rather than validated demand.

## 1. What makes a contract—and what does not

### 1.1 Contract, agreement, order, policy, and record

| Object | Practical meaning | When it creates obligations | Operational consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Agreement** | The parties' bargain or manifested understanding. It can be oral, written, electronic, implied by conduct, or spread across documents. | Only when the governing law's formation and enforceability requirements are met. | A repository that indexes only signed files can miss binding oral/conduct-based commitments, click-through terms, incorporated documents, and later variations. |
| **Contract** | The legally enforceable rights, duties, remedies, and risk allocation resulting from the agreement plus applicable law. | When formation requirements, authority, certainty, legality, and any transaction-specific formalities are satisfied. | The “contract” can be broader than the visible document and narrower than every statement in it if a term is invalid or non-binding. |
| **Purchase order / order form** | A transaction document specifying product/service, quantity, price, term, and delivery under its own terms or a master agreement. | It may itself be an offer, acceptance, or contract; UCC §2-206 says an order to buy goods can invite acceptance by promise or shipment. Under an MSA, it usually activates scoped commercial obligations subject to precedence rules. | Systems must know whether an order stands alone or belongs under an MSA and which terms prevail in a “battle of forms.” |
| **Policy** | A unilaterally issued rule or operating standard (security, travel, HR, supplier code). | Normally not a bilateral contract by itself, but it may become contractually binding through incorporation, assent, course of dealing, or contract language. UK employment terms can appear in a handbook, offer letter, collective agreement, or implied term. | Treat policies as governed references with versions and effective dates, not automatically as contracts or automatically as non-binding. |
| **Statement / notice / acknowledgement** | A communication that records facts or triggers a contractual mechanism: notice of breach, renewal, acceptance, assignment, price change, or termination. | Its legal effect depends on the governing agreement and compliance with the notice clause, timing, method, address, and authority. | Notices need evidence of content, sender, recipient, method, and time; storing the base contract alone is insufficient. |

**FACT — High confidence.** The US UCC definition makes the agreement/contract distinction explicit, and UCC Article 2 allows formation by conduct even when the exact moment is unknown or some terms remain open. An order to buy goods can invite acceptance by a prompt promise to ship or shipment. [UCC §1-201](https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/1/1-201); [UCC §2-204](https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/2/2-204); [UCC §2-206](https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/2/2-206)

**FACT — High confidence.** In England and Wales, the UK Supreme Court held in *RTS Flexible Systems v Müller* that parties who started and substantially performed work after a letter of intent could have a binding agreement despite no final signed contract; the question depended on their communications, conduct, and objective intention. [UK Supreme Court judgment and press summary, 10 March 2010](https://www.supremecourt.uk/cases/uksc-2009-0048)

**FACT — High confidence.** UK employment guidance says terms can be written, verbal, found in a handbook or offer letter, required by law, collectively agreed, or implied. The Supreme Court has also explained that an individual employment contract may incorporate a term from a collective agreement when the employer and employee intend that result. [GOV.UK employment contract terms, accessed 2026-08-19](https://www.gov.uk/employment-contracts-and-conditions/contract-terms); [Secretary of State for DEFRA v PCS Union, UKSC 2023-0075](https://www.supremecourt.uk/cases/judgments/uksc-2023-0075)

### 1.2 Formation, formality, authority, and signature

**FACT — High confidence.** A signature is powerful evidence of assent and often an internal control, but it is not universally required for contract formation. Conversely, particular transactions do require formality: US copyright ownership transfers generally require a signed writing; contracts for sale or disposition of land in England and Wales must satisfy signed-writing requirements; deeds have execution formalities. [17 U.S.C. §204](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/204); [Law of Property (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act 1989, §2](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1989/34/pdfs/ukpga_19890034_en.pdf)

**FACT — High confidence.** Electronic form alone does not invalidate a US interstate/foreign-commerce contract under the E-SIGN Act. The Law Commission concluded that an electronic signature can execute a document in England and Wales when the signer intends to authenticate it and any other formalities are met; witnessing or transaction-specific requirements still matter. [15 U.S.C. §7001](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/7001); [Law Commission electronic execution project, report published 2019](https://lawcom.gov.uk/project/electronic-execution-of-documents/)

**INFERENCE — High confidence.** A contract system needs separate fields for: legal formation status, internal approval status, signature/execution status, effective date, commencement date, and operational activation. Collapsing them into a single “signed” state creates false certainty.

### 1.3 The agreement-family model

A useful model is:

```text
Relationship / counterparty
└── Master or framework agreement
    ├── service agreement / SOW / order form / PO
    │   ├── specification and pricing schedule
    │   ├── SLA / KPI schedule
    │   └── acceptance records and invoices
    ├── DPA / security exhibit / BAA
    ├── amendment / waiver / change order
    ├── renewal, notice, claim, or dispute record
    └── termination and transition records
```

**FACT — High confidence.** The Sabre–DXC MSA provides a concrete enterprise example: the master terms govern service agreements; schedules define services, charges, software, assets, reports, deliverables and service levels; exhibits cover account governance, change control, acceptance tests, security, privacy, insurance and policies; international agreements handle local-law differences; written signed modifications control changes; and termination assistance governs exit. [SEC exhibit, 2021](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1597033/000159703321000022/exhibit10103masterservices.htm)

**INFERENCE — High confidence.** Post-signature extraction at the isolated-document level is unsafe when an amendment changes a deadline, an order inherits an MSA liability cap, a DPA adds incident duties, or a notice exercises a right. Aakd must resolve provenance, precedence, effective periods, and supersession before presenting an action as governing truth.

## 2. Taxonomy: classify by business relationship, economic model, and control burden

The categories overlap. A cloud services deal may be sell-side, subscription, IP licence, DPA, and regulated outsourcing at once. The point of taxonomy is workflow routing—not forcing every contract into one folder.

| Domain | Common archetypes | Core business bargain | High-value fields / obligations | Typical owner and reviewers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Sell-side / revenue** | MSA, SaaS/subscription agreement, order form, SOW, licence, reseller/referral, distribution, sponsorship, customer NDA | Customer pays for goods, software, services, access, outcomes, or rights | scope, price, usage/units, term, renewal, acceptance, SLA, credits, invoicing, revenue recognition, data/security, warranty, liability, termination | Sales/Account Executive; Deal Desk/RevOps; Legal; Finance/Revenue; Security/Privacy; delivery/customer success |
| **Buy-side / procurement** | Supplier MSA, PO, SaaS order, professional-services SOW, equipment purchase, logistics, facilities, outsourcing | Company obtains goods/services while controlling spend, performance, supply and third-party risk | budget, PO, service levels, delivery, insurance, data access, security, subcontractors, audit, business continuity, price changes, renewals, exit assistance | Requester/business owner; Procurement; Finance; Legal; Security/Privacy; IT; risk/compliance; AP |
| **Corporate / governance** | shareholder/investor agreements, board consents, financing documents, intercompany, M&A, asset/share purchase, joint venture, partnership | Ownership, control, capital, strategic transactions, governance rights | authority, cap table, conditions precedent, representations, indemnity, closing deliverables, covenants, consents, disclosure schedules, survival | Board/secretary; CEO/CFO; Legal; investors; tax; finance; external counsel |
| **Employment / workforce** | offer letter, employment agreement, contractor/consultancy, secondment, collective agreement, equity/option, separation/release, contingent-worker SOW | Labour/services in exchange for pay/benefits; status, control, confidentiality and IP | start date, role, pay, hours, benefits, probation, notice, restrictive covenants, IP/confidentiality, policy incorporation, immigration, worker classification | Hiring manager; HR/People Ops; payroll/finance; Legal; executive/signatory; IT/security onboarding |
| **Data, security, and IP** | NDA, DPA, BAA, SCC/addendum, data sharing, software/content licence, IP assignment, research/collaboration, source-code escrow | Permission and limits for information, personal data, technology and creative assets | purpose/use, data categories, roles, safeguards, incident notice, subprocessors, transfers, deletion/return, audit, ownership, licence scope, territory, exclusivity, royalties, infringement | Privacy/DPO; Security; Legal/IP; product/engineering; data owner; compliance |
| **Real estate / assets** | lease, licence to occupy, sale/purchase, property management, construction, equipment lease | Right to occupy/use/transfer an asset and allocate physical/financial risk | property, term, rent, deposit, repair, insurance, service charge, access, alterations, title, conditions, milestones, retainage, change orders | Facilities/operations; real-estate or project team; Finance; Legal; insurers; lenders |
| **Finance / treasury** | loan, credit facility, note, guarantee, security agreement, derivative, bank services, insurance | Provision and price of capital or risk transfer | principal, rate, fees, repayment, financial covenants, collateral, reporting, events of default, cure, acceleration, guarantees, notices | CFO/treasury; board; finance; Legal; lender; auditor; risk |
| **Regulated / public-sector** | government procurement, healthcare BAA, financial-services outsourcing, defence, clinical trial, public grant | Ordinary bargain plus mandatory statutory, regulatory, audit, reporting, flow-down, or public-law controls | eligibility, mandatory clauses, records, audit, data/security, subcontract flow-downs, service continuity, regulator access, transparency, termination | Contracting officer/regulator-facing owner; compliance; security/privacy; Legal; finance; operational owner |

**FACT — High confidence.** Contract type changes who bears cost and performance risk. The US Federal Acquisition Regulation groups contracts broadly into fixed-price and cost-reimbursement categories, with firm-fixed-price placing maximum cost responsibility on the contractor and cost-reimbursement requiring greater surveillance and adequate accounting resources. [FAR Part 16, effective 13 March 2026](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/part-16)

**FACT — High confidence.** Data-processing contracts are not generic addenda. UK GDPR Article 28 contracts must state the subject, duration, nature and purpose of processing, data and data-subject categories, and controller rights, and must cover instructions, confidentiality, security, subprocessors, data-subject rights, assistance, end-of-contract treatment and audits. HIPAA BAAs similarly require written limits, safeguards, incident reporting, subcontractor flow-downs, return/destruction and termination rights. [UK ICO Article 28 guidance, under review following the Data (Use and Access) Act, accessed 2026-08-19](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/accountability-and-governance/contracts-and-liabilities-between-controllers-and-processors-multi/what-needs-to-be-included-in-the-contract/); [US HHS BAA provisions, 2013](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/covered-entities/sample-business-associate-agreement-provisions/index.html)

**FACT — High confidence.** Customer contracts also drive accounting: IFRS 15 requires entities to identify the customer contract and distinct performance obligations, determine and allocate transaction price, and recognise revenue when/as obligations are satisfied. [IFRS Foundation, IFRS 15 overview, accessed 2026-08-19](https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/list-of-standards/ifrs-15-revenue-from-contracts-with-customers/)

**FACT — High confidence.** The UK Government standard says mobilisation should establish roles, risk/issues, contingencies, exit, re-procurement/extension, tools, performance monitoring and budget/spend controls; the contract-management plan is produced by the contract manager and approved by the senior business owner. [GovS 008 Commercial, §5.4, updated 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/government-functional-standard-govs-008-commercial-and-commercial-continuous-improvement-assessment-framework/government-functional-standard-govs-008-commercial-html)

**FACT — High confidence.** Public-sector closeout illustrates why “expired” is not “closed”: FAR closeout checks final invoices, excess funds, audits, subcontracts, property, patents/royalties and completion statements. [FAR 4.804-5, effective 13 March 2026](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/4.804-5)

**INFERENCE — High confidence.** The central post-signature data object should be an **action**, not a date: `{governing source; exact text; party; owner; beneficiary; action/right; trigger; condition; due/window; approval; dependencies; evidence; status; exception; escalation; reviewer; version}`. A date-only reminder cannot model “within 72 hours after confirmed incident,” “renew unless notice 90 days before,” “invoice after customer acceptance,” or “delete data at termination unless legally retained.”

**OPEN GAP.** No public evidence establishes which subset of these fields creates enough value for Aakd's ICP to pay, or how much reviewer correction burden is tolerable. That requires representative agreement families and observed operating episodes.

## 4. How the work changes with company size

For this memo, **small = 1–50 employees**, **medium = 51–500**, and **enterprise = more than 500**. These are research bands, not universal legal or market definitions. Aakd's provisional ICP spans the medium band, with 51–200 and 201–500 retained separately in validation.

| Dimension | Small (1–50) | Medium (51–500) | Enterprise (>500) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Typical ownership** | Founder/owner, COO, HR or finance lead; legal often external or absent | First in-house counsel, legal/contract manager, RevOps/Deal Desk, procurement or functional operations; lean teams cover several roles | Separate legal, legal ops, procurement, deal desk, privacy, security, tax, revenue/accounting and contract-management roles |
| **Intake** | Email, chat, shared drive; the requester may also approve/sign | CRM/procurement form or ticket plus email/Word; thresholds decide reviews | Structured intake by contract family, entity, region, value and risk; service levels and queues |
| **Templates / negotiation** | Few templates; third-party paper is common; founder/external lawyer handles exceptions | Template suite and playbook emerge; business self-service for routine work; legal handles deviations | Many jurisdiction/product/entity templates, specialist clauses and negotiated customer/supplier paper |
| **Approval and authority** | Informal or founder-centric; authority may be understood rather than documented | Spend/revenue/risk thresholds; functional and executive approvals; delegated authority begins | Formal authorization matrix, segregation of duties, board/reserved matters and audit evidence |
| **Systems** | Email, Word/PDF, e-sign, Drive/Dropbox, calendar, accounting/HR | CRM + e-sign + shared storage + procurement/ERP/HRIS + spreadsheets; integration gaps are common | CLM/repository integrated with CRM, procurement, ERP, GRC, IAM, service/project and BI systems |
| **Post-signature** | Human memory and calendar; owner may be the signer | Responsibility fragments across legal/ops/finance/delivery; obligation spreadsheets and renewal calendars emerge | Dedicated contract/vendor/customer managers, controls, scorecards, audits and formal change/exit processes—but scale creates data-quality and adoption problems |
| **Main failure shape** | No process, missing document, unauthorized promise, forgotten deadline | Handoff and system fragmentation; lean owner becomes bottleneck; no complete agreement family | Control complexity, duplicate systems, inconsistent global processes, legacy migration and unclear local/global precedence |
| **Proportionate solution** | Simple templates, e-sign, repository and reminders; full CLM can be excessive | Rapid intake, family-aware repository, reviewed action handoff and targeted integrations | Configurable governance, enterprise integrations, advanced access, migration, analytics and implementation capacity |

**FACT — High confidence.** The UK Government's commercial standard explicitly says simple low-cost contracts may need no commercial-specialist support, while complex contracts should confirm a senior business owner, contract manager, commercial specialists and other roles during mobilisation. [GovS 008, §5.4.1](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/government-functional-standard-govs-008-commercial-and-commercial-continuous-improvement-assessment-framework/government-functional-standard-govs-008-commercial-html)

**FACT — Medium confidence.** ACC's maturity model says contract-management priorities and target maturity vary by department size, staffing and budget; its contract-management progression runs from ad hoc review toward defined ownership and increasingly integrated/managed practice. It is a practitioner benchmarking tool, not proof that every company follows the same progression. [ACC Legal Operations Maturity Model 2.0, republished 2025](https://www.acc.com/resource-library/acc-legal-operations-maturity-model-0); [ACC model PDF](https://www.acc.com/sites/default/files/program-materials/upload/Legal-Opertaitons-Maturity-Model-2.0---ACC.pdf)

**INFERENCE — Medium confidence.** The 50–500 band is structurally attractive when contract complexity has outgrown founder memory and shared folders, but headcount has not funded a mature legal-operations programme. Headcount alone is a weak qualifier: contract volume, agreement-family complexity, regulated data, recurring triggers, and a funded owner matter more.

## 5. Named company examples: what is actually documented

These examples are **illustrative, not representative**. Small-company public evidence is particularly sparse and vendor-mediated. Workflow claims below stay within what the linked source documents.

### 5.1 Small companies (two documented cases)

#### SFG — 1–10 employees, Denmark; employment contracting

**FACT — Medium confidence; vendor case study.** Contractbook classifies SFG, a cleaning-services company, as 1–10 employees. Its customer story says the HR manager replaced a paper-heavy employment-contract process with a digital workflow and reduced per-employee contract handling from roughly two hours to about ten minutes. This documents a high-frequency HR workflow owned by a functional operator, not a broad CLM deployment. The timing and savings are seller-published and not independently audited. [Contractbook / SFG customer story, accessed 2026-08-19](https://contractbook.com/customers/sfg)

**INFERENCE — Medium confidence.** The value driver is repetitive generation and onboarding throughput. A complex obligation-intelligence system would likely be disproportionate unless SFG has additional contract families or compliance consequences that the case study does not disclose.

#### Burd Delivery — 11–50 employees, Denmark; worker onboarding

**FACT — Medium confidence; vendor case study.** Contractbook classifies Burd Delivery as 11–50 employees. The described flow begins when a recruit submits a web form: data populates a centrally controlled employment template; a human remains in the loop; signature triggers onboarding information and creation in the payroll system; executed contracts are centrally indexed. The story attributes at least 15 minutes saved per recruit to the workflow. [Contractbook / Burd Delivery customer story, accessed 2026-08-19](https://contractbook.com/customers/burd-delivery)

**INFERENCE — High confidence.** This is a contract-triggered business process: execution is the event that authorizes downstream onboarding and payroll creation. The operational gain comes from validated structured data and event automation more than clause analytics.

**OPEN GAP.** Neither small-company case is US/UK, and both are seller-published. They establish that small firms can have high-frequency contract workflows, but not that US/UK 1–50 employee firms will fund Aakd's post-signature wedge.

### 5.2 Medium companies (four documented cases)

#### Expo Pass — 50+ employees, Chicago, US; sell-side contracting

**FACT — Medium confidence; vendor case study.** Juro classifies Expo Pass as 50+ and says the company grew from a handful of employees in 2018 to more than 50 in 2023. The case describes a file-centric e-sign/PDF process replaced by contract generation, approvals, execution, tracking and Salesforce-linked storage; uploaded counterparty PDFs can be tracked and the final version stored against Salesforce. It identifies the COO as an early process owner. [Juro / Expo Pass case study, accessed 2026-08-19](https://juro.com/case-studies/expo-pass)

**Limitation.** “50+” proves the company crossed the medium threshold at the stated time but does not establish its precise 2026 headcount. The vendor's “business-critical” outcome quote is customer-attributed, not independently measured.

#### Tibber — documented growth from 30 to 330; cross-functional repository and renewals

**FACT — Medium confidence; vendor case study.** Juro reports Tibber grew from 30 to 330 employees in two years and hired a sole Head of Legal to build legal infrastructure. Contracts were in shared drives, email and personal desktops; the lawyer spent time searching and could not reliably track deadlines or renewals, while the CFO and Head of Quality also needed visibility. Active contracts running through e-sign affected partnerships, e-commerce, marketing and HR. [Juro / Tibber case study, accessed 2026-08-19](https://juro.com/case-studies/tibber)

**INFERENCE — High confidence.** Tibber closely illustrates the proposed ICP transition: operational need reaches finance and quality, yet a sole lawyer is asked to create the system. The documented pain is retrieval and lifecycle visibility; the public story does not quantify missed-renewal loss or prove willingness to pay specifically for owned post-signature actions.

#### Placemakr — 240+ employees, Washington, DC, US; sales/hospitality contracting

**FACT — Medium confidence; vendor case study.** Juro classifies Placemakr as 240+ employees. Its sales and hospitality teams used HubSpot but moved across six tools to generate contracts. The company says the HubSpot integration and consolidated templates reduced manual edits, data entry and template search, and estimates a 50% reduction in time spent on contract creation/management; it also gained stage-level structured data. [Juro / Placemakr case study, accessed 2026-08-19](https://juro.com/case-studies/placemakr)

**Limitation.** The time reduction is a customer estimate in vendor marketing, not a controlled measurement. It principally supports pre-signature self-service and CRM continuity, not Aakd's post-signature-action wedge.

#### Lenus — 201–500 employees, Denmark; sales, HR and finance handoffs

**FACT — Medium confidence; vendor case study.** Contractbook classifies Lenus as 201–500 employees and reports more than 10,000 annual contracts. The documented sell-side flow uses Salesforce stage data and a questionnaire to generate a country-specific agreement; the story says 95% proceed without legal touch. After signature, metadata is added to a data sheet and finance receives bank, contact and payment terms. Separately, Greenhouse and HiBob data generate country-specific employment contracts. [Contractbook / Lenus customer story, accessed 2026-08-19](https://contractbook.com/customers/lenus)

**INFERENCE — High confidence.** Lenus demonstrates two different archetypes requiring different triggers and destinations: sell-side execution informs finance, while hiring data initiates employment documents. It supports integration and governed self-service, but the extreme volume and highly standardised agreements may not resemble lower-volume, higher-complexity ICP customers.

### 5.3 Enterprise companies (three documented cases)

#### GitLab — 2,375 team members at 2025-01-31; public sell-side and procurement workflows

**FACT — High confidence; operator-authored handbook plus SEC size basis.** GitLab's FY2025 Form 10-K reports approximately 2,375 team members in 60 countries. Its public handbook documents the quote-to-cash path: Salesforce legal requests create assigned cases; versions and communications stay in the case; legal reviews template/customer paper above stated opportunity thresholds; an executable PDF gets a legal approval stamp; only authorized signatories may sign; DocuSign routes execution; Deal Desk, revenue, legal, order management and finance add reviews for non-standard paper before booking and licence fulfilment. [GitLab FY2025 Form 10-K, filed 21 March 2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1653482/000162828025014344/gtlb-20250131.htm); [GitLab sales order processing handbook, accessed 2026-08-19](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/sales/field-operations/order-processing/)

**FACT — High confidence; operator-authored handbook.** On buy-side work, GitLab requires procurement engagement before terms are agreed or work begins; uses Zip for requests, renewals, changes and non-renewal notices; routes privacy, security and compliance reviews based on data/services; uses bid and negotiation thresholds; stores executed vendor contracts in Zip; and conducts quarterly rolling renewal reviews with business owners, beginning renewal work at least 90 days ahead. [GitLab procurement handbook, accessed 2026-08-19](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/finance/procurement/); [GitLab contract storage handbook, updated 2025](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/legal/vendor-contract-filing-process/)

**INFERENCE — High confidence.** GitLab shows contracts acting as cross-system control gates: the approved version governs signature, the executed document gates revenue booking and fulfilment, and the vendor contract gates work, onboarding and payment. It also demonstrates why a generic “contract status” cannot replace role-specific controls.

#### Sabre and DXC — enterprise outsourcing agreement family

**FACT — High confidence; SEC-filed executed contract.** Sabre reported 4,650 employees at 2025-12-31. Its amended and restated MSA with DXC is direct evidence of an enterprise services relationship organised through master terms, service agreements, schedules, local-country agreements and change orders. It assigns governance, procedures, service levels/credits, reports, acceptance tests, security/privacy, continuity, invoices, disputed charges, IP, amendments, dispute resolution and termination assistance. [Sabre 2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1597033/000162828026008800/sabr-20251231.htm); [Sabre–DXC MSA, SEC exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1597033/000159703321000022/exhibit10103masterservices.htm)

**INFERENCE — High confidence.** This agreement family would overwhelm a flat document/reminder model. Operational truth depends on hierarchy, country, service, schedule, change order, procedure and time. It is good evidence for Aakd's domain architecture, but it is poor evidence for the initial 50–500 go-to-market segment because its implementation burden is enterprise-grade.

#### Cardinal Infrastructure Group — enterprise construction contract operations

**FACT — High confidence; SEC filing.** Cardinal's 2025 Form 10-K describes monthly contract loss reviews considering performance, site conditions, profitability, claims and change orders; unapproved change orders affect when revenue and cost can be recognised; retainage and billing/progress timing create contract assets and liabilities. The company disclosed $135,647, $4,039,488 and $803,912 of loss-contract provisions for 2025, 2024 and 2023 respectively, and explained a $37.2 million year-over-year increase in contract assets in 2025. [Cardinal Infrastructure 2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2079999/000119312526120014/cdnl-20251231.htm); [filed annual report, 24 April 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2079999/000119312526177474/2025_annual_report.pdf)

**INFERENCE — High confidence.** Construction turns contract management into continuous financial and project control: scope and acceptance evidence determine change-order recovery, billing, revenue, cash and loss recognition. A renewal reminder would be largely irrelevant; change authority and evidence are the wedge.

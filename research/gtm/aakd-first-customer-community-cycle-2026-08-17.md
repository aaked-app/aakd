## 1. Executive decision

Aakd should not wait for a larger GitHub audience before learning from customers. It should run two connected funnels:

- **Customer evidence: 70% of GTM effort.** Find a painful, repeated post-signature contract workflow, confirm its buyer, and obtain real corpora and payment evidence.
- **OSS community proof: 25%.** Make independent self-hosting, first value, contribution and trust observable.
- **Measurement and synthesis: 5%.** Keep facts, inferences and unknowns separate; feed evidence back to product, coding, UX and research sessions.

GitHub stars are an awareness signal, not the objective. The customer objective is repeated paid use. The OSS objective is independent activation and contribution.

## 2. What Aakd is testing

### Product thesis

Aakd is an open-source, self-hostable, AI-native contract operating system. It should help people and authorized agents turn agreements into reviewed facts, obligations, decisions and safe actions. Its strongest intended differentiation is the combination of:

1. a simple contract-first experience;
2. exact citations, confidence and human review;
3. agreement-family and precedence intelligence;
4. safe, contract-native recipes;
5. governed access for agents;
6. self-hosting and data portability;
7. a zero-operations managed Cloud path.

No single item above is unique. The compound experience and trust model must be proven.

### Customer hypothesis

The selected validation cohort is:

- a 50–500 employee US or UK B2B service, software or technical organization;
- roughly 100–2,000 active customer or vendor agreements;
- recurring MSAs, SOWs, order forms, amendments, renewals or service commitments;
- no mature legal-operations function;
- agreements and actions fragmented across Drive or SharePoint, CRM, e-signature, spreadsheets, calendars and work tools;
- a recent concrete event such as a missed notice, renewal surprise, unowned obligation, billing or delivery mismatch, audit, acquisition or volume jump.

“Startup,” “SMB” and “needs contracts” are not sufficient qualification.

### Separate OSS cohort

The likely OSS adopter is a self-hoster, technical operator, privacy-sensitive team, legal-tech builder or implementation partner. OSS adoption can create trust, distribution and contributions, but it is not evidence of Cloud willingness to pay.

## 4. Account qualification scorecard

Score from public evidence before identifying or contacting a person. A claim about pain is never inferred from company type.

| Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Size | Outside 50–500 | Size unclear or borderline | Verified 50–500 |
| Repeatable agreements | No evidence | Plausible | Explicit recurring contracts, SOWs, retainers or managed delivery |
| Post-signature complexity | Simple/one-off | Multiple projects or entities | Observable obligations, SLAs, milestones, amendments or regulated delivery |
| Fragmentation signal | None | Multiple systems likely | Publicly described handoffs, reporting or reconciliation |
| Trigger | None | Growth/change only | Recent incident, acquisition, audit, escalation or contract-volume jump |
| Owner reachability | No plausible owner | Role exists | Current operational owner and direct professional route verified |
| Incumbent risk | Mature system likely solves it | Unknown | Clear reason to test a gap despite current tooling |

- **A: 11–14:** research the current owner after suppression and evidence checks.
- **B: 8–10:** keep as a comparison account; resolve missing facts first.
- **C: 0–7:** do not contact.

Automatic disqualifiers include: few agreements and no consequence, AI-review-only need, immediate global-CLM replacement, mature incumbent coverage, no recurring workflow, or no operational owner.

## 6. Discovery conversation

The objective is to falsify the hypothesis, not present a demo. Ask for the most recent event first.

1. “Tell me about the last contract commitment or notice that created avoidable work, delay or cost after signature.”
2. “Where did the agreement, deadline and owner each live at that moment?”
3. “What did the team do, and what was the consequence in time, money, delay or risk?”
4. “Why did the current CLM, SharePoint setup, PSA, ATS, CRM or project tool not prevent it?”
5. “Who owns this result operationally, and who can fund a remedy?”
6. “If 25 redacted agreements became reviewed, source-linked actions, what would happen next?”
7. “Would this be a one-time cleanup or a repeated operating cycle?”
8. “Would you choose self-hosted Aakd or managed Aakd Cloud? What practical reason drives that choice?”
9. “What would make Aakd a non-starter?”

Do not ask for documents in first contact. Do not equate interest, a booked call or praise with validation.

### Evidence captured after every conversation

- exact participant role and relationship to the workflow;
- agreement family and concrete event;
- current systems and claimed limitation;
- direct quote, separated from interviewer interpretation;
- quantified consequence or `unknown`;
- operational owner and economic buyer path;
- next recurring batch or proof that it is one-time;
- self-hosted, managed or neither preference and reason;
- willingness to provide a safely redacted corpus;
- willingness to prepay an identical diagnostic;
- disconfirming evidence and incumbent coverage.

## 7. The diagnostic offer

Do not lead with this offer. Introduce it only after a qualifying conversation and separate commercial authorization.

### Input

Twenty-five redacted, executed agreements from one comparable workflow. No production credentials, autonomous external action or legal-advice promise.

### Output

A human-reviewed action ledger containing:

- proposed action or obligation;
- exact source text and page;
- trigger, due date or notice window;
- confidence and unresolved ambiguity;
- proposed owner;
- review result: confirmed, corrected or unable to determine;
- export destination or next approved step.

### Success

The customer judges at least 80% of proposed actions useful enough to assign, correct or export, requests a second comparable cycle, and explicitly chooses managed Cloud for an operational reason if Cloud is the intended purchase.

### Stop conditions

- A normal configuration of the incumbent already creates the result.
- The need is only storage, reminders, generic AI chat or drafting.
- There is no buyer or repeated next batch.
- Safe redaction and handling cannot be agreed.
- Value depends on autonomous legal judgment, unsupported compliance claims or unavailable integrations.

## 8. First-contact rules

First contact is a research invitation, not a pitch.

- One researched person per company.
- Use one verified professional observation and ask one workflow question.
- Never claim the company has a problem.
- Ask for a short reply or optional 15-minute conversation.
- No product, pricing, pilot, document, access or scarcity request.
- No tracking pixels, hidden redirects or purchased/list-scraped personal emails.
- Stop on decline, opt-out, complaint, bounce or evidence that the workflow is already covered.
- A human approves every recipient and exact message before sending.

## 9. Community motion

Community work must turn the repository into proof, not manufacture attention.

### Readiness before promotion

1. Two people outside the core team independently install Aakd from the public instructions.
2. Both reach the first cited, reviewed contract action without live help.
3. Installation failures and time-to-value are recorded honestly.
4. The README states what works, what is experimental and what is not yet supported.
5. Security reporting, contribution guidance, roadmap and issue templates are current.
6. Three small contributor tasks have a reproducible problem, expected behavior, boundaries and acceptance criteria.

### Content briefs

These are briefs, not approved posts.

1. **Build-in-public proof:** “What an AI-native CLM must do after extraction.” Show exact citations, human review and the difference between a fact and a safe action.
2. **Honest benchmark:** publish a versioned synthetic/adversarial corpus, evaluation method, errors and corrections. Avoid unsupported accuracy headlines.
3. **Agent trust architecture:** demonstrate scoped agent access, redaction, confirmation before external actions and audit evidence.
4. **Contributor invitation:** ask for one bounded connector, extractor or domain recipe with a clear maintainer response promise.
5. **Customer learning:** share an anonymized, aggregated workflow pattern only after consent and enough examples to avoid identifying a company.

### Distribution surfaces after approval

- GitHub repository, Discussions and release notes;
- Hacker News for a genuine technical artifact or launch milestone;
- relevant self-hosted, legal-operations and developer communities, following each community’s rules;
- founder LinkedIn only after clearance;
- technical articles that answer real self-hosting, security and agent-integration questions.

Never buy stars, run engagement pods, mass-post identical copy or treat a launch spike as retention.

## 11. Decision rules

### Continue the selected ICP when

- at least five comparable organizations describe the same recent episode;
- two economic owners prepay the same diagnostic;
- both request a second cycle;
- managed Cloud is preferred for a concrete operational reason;
- delivery does not require bespoke consulting for every account.

### Narrow or change the ICP when

- pain clusters around one agreement family, sector or role;
- the buyer differs consistently from the user;
- US and UK evidence materially diverges;
- a specialist system solves the problem in one cohort but not another.

### Stop the commercial hypothesis when

- interview interest does not convert to corpora and prepayment;
- the workflow is one-time cleanup;
- incumbent configuration solves it;
- customers want self-hosting only and no managed value remains;
- safe, reviewable outcomes cannot be produced reliably.

Do not stop the OSS product solely because the first Cloud hypothesis fails. Separate community utility from managed-Cloud economics.

## 12. Cross-session handoffs

### Coding

Only accept customer-derived work with a linked episode, evidence count and target outcome. Immediate Phase-0 asks are measurable activation, an adversarial corpus, a unified cited-action journey and truthful capability status—not additional breadth.

### UI/UX

Test whether a new operator can move from upload to a reviewed, owned action without understanding CLM terminology. Preserve progressive disclosure for advanced workflows.

### Research

Verify incumbents against official documentation and current customer evidence. Mark every statement `FACT`, `INFERENCE` or `UNKNOWN`.

### Security

Define and test permission-aware retrieval, read/write separation, redaction policies, confirmation gates, auditability, revocation and prompt-injection boundaries for agents.

### Community/content

Publish artifacts and learning, not generic launch claims. Every public claim needs a current reproducible proof or explicit experimental label.

## 14. Truth and governance rules

- `PRODUCT.md` controls product direction. Earlier research documents are evidence, not current strategy when they conflict with it.
- No simulated ICP interview counts as customer evidence. An ICP agent may critique questions or generate hypotheses only.
- Public social posts and anonymous comments are discovery signals, not willingness-to-pay proof.
- A company’s business model is evidence of workflow surface, not evidence of pain.
- A person’s title is not evidence of economic authority.
- No contract text, personal data or secrets enter research notes without a defined, consented handling boundary.
- No automated message, post, follow-up or external product action is allowed in this cycle.

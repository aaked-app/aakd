# How early SaaS companies get customers — and what Papermark-style virality actually means for Aakd

**Status:** desk research  
**Date:** 2026-08-17  
**Scope:** first customers, open-source distribution, Papermark, Cal.com, PostHog and Supabase  
**Decision use:** Aakd Phase-0 GTM and community motion

## Executive conclusion

There is no single “normal” SaaS acquisition channel. The repeated early-stage pattern is:

1. founders recruit and qualify initial users manually;
2. they deliver value and onboarding personally;
3. they learn a precise problem, buyer and language;
4. a launch or community amplifies a simple, credible story;
5. the product captures demand through a low-friction path;
6. content, SEO, referrals, partnerships and sales compound only after the initial proof.

Open-source attention and customer acquisition overlap, but they are not the same funnel. Stars can create trust, reach and contributor interest. Revenue normally comes from a distinct reason to pay: hosted convenience, team collaboration, security controls, support, compliance, scale or reduced operational burden.

The implication for Aakd is not “choose customer conversations or virality.” It is to use direct customer work to produce the proof, language and artifacts that make a public launch worth sharing.

## 1. How SaaS normally gets its first customers

### Before product-market fit

The most reliable pattern is founder-led and intentionally manual:

- warm introductions and the founders’ professional network;
- individually researched outbound to a narrow customer hypothesis;
- direct participation in the communities where the problem is already discussed;
- concierge onboarding or a manually delivered diagnostic;
- early-adopter referrals;
- product launches as bursts of discovery, not the whole acquisition system.

[Paul Graham’s “Do Things That Don’t Scale”](https://www.paulgraham.com/ds.html) says the most common early unscalable act is manually recruiting users. [Stripe Atlas’s first-ten-customers guide](https://stripe.com/in/guides/atlas/starting-sales) similarly recommends a small, founder-built prospect list, prioritizing buyers who already adopt new software and can approve a purchase with a light process.

The first ten customers are primarily a learning and sales problem. Automating outbound, hiring salespeople or buying broad traffic before the founder can reliably qualify and close customers usually scales uncertainty.

### After an initial repeatable pattern

SaaS companies gradually add channels that can compound:

- customer referrals and case studies;
- SEO and problem-specific content;
- integrations and ecosystem partnerships;
- templates, calculators and useful free tools;
- self-serve product-led acquisition;
- repeatable outbound or account-based sales;
- paid acquisition only when conversion, retention and unit economics are understood.

The correct mix depends on buyer, annual contract value, implementation burden and whether one user can adopt the product without organization-wide permission.

### For enterprise software

Founder-led qualification remains necessary, but the real funnel includes security, privacy, legal, procurement, implementation and adoption. A signed contract is not the finish line. A customer that never reaches recurring value is not successful acquisition.

For Aakd, this means early Cloud customers must be organizations where one credible champion can expose the workflow, involve the buyer, agree a safe data boundary and move through a relatively light purchasing process.

## 2. Papermark: reconstructed traction story

### What is documented

Papermark started with a narrow, instantly understandable position: an **open-source DocSend alternative** with analytics and custom domains.

- Founder Marc Seitz reported that an initial idea tweet received **265 likes**. A weekend build followed; the launch tweet reportedly received **450 likes and more than 95,000 views**. [Founder interview](https://www.openpioneers.com/p/papermark)
- Papermark's own launch retrospective says it sought feedback from **10–20 people before launch**, prepared for a week, and assembled a supporter list of roughly **20–30 meaningful connections**. [Papermark launch retrospective](https://www.papermark.com/de/blog/product-hunt-launch)
- Papermark launched on Product Hunt on 4 September 2023 and is recorded as **#1 product of the day**, with approximately **800 votes and 246 comments** on the current page. [Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/papermark-2)
- Its retrospective recorded **850 upvotes, 250 comments, 4,000 visits, 2,000 unique visitors, 250 new GitHub stars and 300 signups**. It also reported warming the audience on X, LinkedIn and Reddit and participating in open-source and build-in-public communities. These are company-reported launch metrics. [Papermark launch retrospective](https://www.papermark.com/de/blog/product-hunt-launch)
- Its October 2023 Show HN post reported more than **1,000 GitHub stars and 10 contributors**; the post itself received **35 points and 15 comments**. [Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=37984167)
- By a January 2024 founder interview, Papermark reported **1,502 stars, 157 forks, 18 contributors and roughly 3,000 users**. [Open Pioneers](https://www.openpioneers.com/p/papermark)
- The team described a continuing growth system: feature articles on DEV, launch weeks, repeated Product Hunt launches, an investor database, country/stage investor lists and a VC GPT. These tools were adjacent to the fundraising users Papermark wanted to attract.
- As of this research, GitHub displays approximately **8.9k stars and 1.4k forks**. [Papermark GitHub](https://github.com/papermark/papermark)
- Papermark currently claims use by **60,000+ companies across 100+ countries**. That is a company claim, not independently audited customer or paid-account data. [Papermark About](https://www.papermark.com/about)
- Marc Seitz publicly claimed **$900k ARR** in 2025 and said revenue had struggled during the first six months. This remains a founder-reported number. [Founder LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/marcseitz_we-struggled-to-grow-revenue-for-the-first-activity-7371188142709850113-4Jr5)

### Why the story spread

Papermark combined several reinforcing factors:

1. **A known incumbent and obvious comparison.** “Open-source DocSend alternative” explained category, opponent and benefit in one line.
2. **A narrow first action.** Upload a document, share a link and see engagement. The value was easy to demo and try.
3. **Founder-market overlap.** Founders already shared pitch decks and were active on X, Product Hunt, Hacker News and GitHub.
4. **Open-source legitimacy.** The repository made privacy, customization and self-hosting claims inspectable.
5. **A prepared audience.** The launch built on earlier posts, direct feedback and community relationships.
6. **Concentrated launches.** Product Hunt, X and HN created overlapping attention bursts.
7. **Adjacent microtools.** Investor-search tools attracted the same users who needed pitch-deck sharing and data rooms.
8. **Continuous public shipping.** The story did not end after one launch.
9. **A clear paid route.** Hosted convenience, team and data-room capabilities provided reasons to pay beyond the repository.

Papermark was not viral merely because it was open source. It packaged a recognizable replacement story for an internet-native audience and repeatedly gave that audience useful things to share.

### What Papermark does not prove

- Product Hunt votes and GitHub stars do not reveal paid conversion or retention.
- The first audience was founders and developers, not an enterprise legal or procurement buying committee.
- A document-sharing product has an externally shared link loop that a private internal CLM does not naturally possess.
- Public company and revenue numbers are mostly self-reported.
- Its current production self-hosting terms require commercial licensing, so “open source” and “unrestricted production self-hosting” should not be treated as identical.

## 3. Comparable open-source stories

### PostHog

PostHog launched as open-source, self-hosted product analytics—a familiar category with a strong privacy/data-control reason. Its Launch HN received **282 points and 83 comments**. The founders manually onboarded early users, used their Slack community to test paid interest, added a pricing page with a founder calendar, and launched PostHog Cloud roughly two months after the HN launch. The founder says Cloud and lower-friction self-service materially improved growth. [Launch HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=22376732), [first 1,000 users](https://newsletter.posthog.com/p/how-we-got-our-first-1000-users)

**Lesson for Aakd:** use OSS to earn technical trust and hosted Cloud to remove operational burden, but keep manual onboarding long enough to learn why someone pays.

### Supabase

Supabase had roughly **80 alpha users** before an early user posted it to Hacker News. The launch produced approximately **30,000 visitors and 1,400 signups in seven days** and increased hosted databases tenfold overnight. Supabase then converted the launch into a repeated Launch Week system, shipping multiple substantial features and community artifacts together. [Alpha launch postmortem](https://supabase.com/blog/alpha-launch-postmortem), [launch process](https://supabase.com/blog/supabase-how-we-launch)

**Lesson for Aakd:** a simple “open-source alternative to X” can unlock discovery, but only when the audience can understand and activate the product quickly. A launch needs operational readiness and a follow-on shipping cadence.

### Cal.com

Cal.com launched as an open-source Calendly alternative and gained attention across Product Hunt, HN, Reddit and GitHub. Within a few months it reported more than **9,000 stars, roughly 900 forks, 79 contributors and a 1,300-member Slack**. Its growth had two structural advantages: scheduling links are naturally shared with non-users, and “Calendly open source” offered strong search intent. [Series A memo](https://cal.com/blog/series-a-memo)

**Lesson for Aakd:** distinguish launch amplification from product virality. Cal.com’s shared booking links expose the product during normal use; Aakd’s confidential contract workflows are mostly invisible. Aakd needs an intentional public artifact loop instead.

### Documenso

Documenso uses the direct “open-source DocuSign alternative” frame and builds trust around inspectable signing infrastructure. More importantly, it created a bounded paid learning cohort: the first 100 early adopters were offered a **$30/month** plan with broad access and roadmap influence. Documenso reported filling those 100 places after 12 months and 13 releases. Its 2025 review later reported **34k users, 340 paying customers, roughly 400k signed documents and 10k GitHub stars**; these are company-reported figures. [Early-adopter offer](https://documenso.com/blog/early-adopters), [cohort completion](https://documenso.com/blog/sunsetting-early-adopters), [2025 review](https://documenso.com/blog/2025-a-year-in-review)

Its repository also demonstrates a maintenance warning: sustaining external contributions is operational work; Documenso currently limits outside pull requests to trusted contributors while continuing to accept detailed issues. [Documenso GitHub](https://github.com/documenso/documenso), [founder rationale](https://documenso.com/blog/why-i-started-documenso)

**Lesson for Aakd:** a capped, paid early-adopter cohort is more relevant to validation than maximizing free signups. Community success also requires maintainer capacity, issue quality and governance; “we accept all contributions” is not a sustainable strategy.

## 4. The shared viral pattern

The successful examples repeatedly combine:

| Ingredient | Papermark | PostHog | Supabase | Cal.com |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Familiar incumbent/category | DocSend | Mixpanel/Amplitude | Firebase | Calendly |
| One-line open alternative | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Technical/self-host audience | Strong | Strong | Strong | Strong |
| Initial users before scale launch | Yes | Yes | ~80 alpha users | Side-project users/community |
| Launch amplifier | X, PH, HN | HN | HN, Launch Weeks | PH, HN, Reddit |
| Recurring content/shipping | Articles, tools, launches | Founder content | Launch Weeks | SEO/community |
| Natural product-sharing loop | Moderate | Weak | Developer projects | Very strong |
| Paid hosted/enterprise route | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |

The causal claim we can safely make is modest: these companies had understandable open-source positioning, activated early users, concentrated public launches and continued distribution afterward. We cannot infer that stars caused revenue.

## 5. What Aakd should copy

### A. Use a sharper public doorway than “full AI-native CLM”

Recommended initial public frame:

> **The open-source contract operating system for reviewed actions and AI agents.**

Supporting proof:

> Upload an agreement. Get source-linked obligations, owners and dates. Review them before a human or authorized agent acts.

Why: it preserves the larger vision while giving people one result they can understand and demonstrate.

### B. Manufacture a legitimate sharing loop

Aakd lacks Cal.com’s natural public-link loop because contracts are confidential. The closest safe alternatives are:

- a public, versioned adversarial contract benchmark;
- shareable synthetic recipe packs;
- an agent/MCP demo using synthetic agreements;
- a “contract operations health check” that stores no uploaded contract;
- redacted aggregate learning from customer research with consent;
- contributor-built extractors, integrations and jurisdiction/industry packs.

Each artifact should point to one clean self-host or Cloud action.

### C. Launch in layers

1. **Proof launch:** benchmark and cited-action journey.
2. **OSS launch:** independent install evidence, truthful README and contributor tasks.
3. **Agent launch:** permission-scoped MCP/API with redaction and confirmation gates.
4. **Cloud launch:** zero-operations hosted path with a clear reason to pay.
5. **Vertical launch:** one validated agreement workflow and customer evidence.

Do not announce all five as one unfocused launch.

### D. Build adjacent tools for the same buyer

Potential Aakd microtools, ranked by fit:

1. contract notice-window calculator using synthetic/manual inputs;
2. contract-workflow maturity assessment;
3. public clause-to-action benchmark explorer;
4. self-hosted CLM deployment estimator;
5. agent-access security checklist for contract repositories.

These should capture a relevant problem, not generic traffic.

### E. Convert attention deliberately

Every launch should distinguish:

- visitor;
- GitHub star;
- installer;
- first cited action;
- repeat active organization;
- qualified customer conversation;
- Cloud trial or diagnostic;
- paying customer;
- external contributor.

Without this funnel, a star spike can look like success while producing no customers or maintainers.

## 6. What Aakd should not copy blindly

- Do not claim to be an “Ironclad alternative” until the comparison is honest at the workflow being shown.
- Do not target founders merely because they are easy to reach; contracts become urgent at specific volume, complexity and consequence thresholds.
- Do not treat Product Hunt ranking as ICP validation.
- Do not launch before two independent users can install and reach the promised result.
- Do not publish accuracy percentages without a versioned corpus and method.
- Do not promise unrestricted production self-hosting if future licensing or support policy differs.
- Do not accept unlimited community scope without maintainer response capacity.
- Do not rely on one viral moment. Each comparable company built a recurring shipping or content system.

## 7. Recommended Aakd acquisition system

### Customer lane

Founder-led research → qualified incident → safe diagnostic → prepayment → repeat cycle → Cloud preference → reference/referral.

### OSS lane

Public proof artifact → repository visit → clean install → cited-action activation → repeat use → issue/recipe/integration → contribution or advocate.

### Bridge between them

- Customer incidents determine benchmark cases and recipes.
- OSS usage reveals installation, trust and extensibility problems.
- Benchmarks and recipes create credible public content.
- Community adopters who want backups, upgrades, support and assurance become Cloud leads.
- Cloud revenue funds maintainership and enterprise-grade security.

## 8. Decision

Continue the previously selected **70% customer / 25% community / 5% measurement** allocation.

Do not attempt a major launch yet. First create the conditions that made the comparable launches work:

1. ten qualified customer conversations;
2. two independent clean installs;
3. one excellent synthetic cited-action demonstration;
4. a truthful, differentiated repository landing experience;
5. a public benchmark or recipe artifact worth discussing;
6. instrumentation separating stars from activation and commercial evidence.

Then launch the narrow proof repeatedly across GitHub, Hacker News, relevant communities and approved founder channels. The goal is not to “go viral.” The goal is to create a repeatable loop from useful public proof to users, learning, contributors and paying Cloud customers.

## Evidence quality note

Product pages, founder interviews and founder posts are primary for what the company claims and did, but not independent audits of revenue, customer count or causality. Product Hunt, Hacker News and GitHub provide stronger observable launch and repository signals. Numerical claims without independent corroboration are labeled as reported or claimed.
